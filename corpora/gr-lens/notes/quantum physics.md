## What this is
Quantum mechanics describes a world where particles exist in superposition, observation affects outcomes, and uncertainty is fundamental. Rather than offering deterministic predictions, it presents a probabilistic framework—challenging classical notions of fixed identity, causality, and locality. As a model of reality, it raises profound questions about the nature of observation, consciousness, and what it means for something to “exist.”
## Why it matters
Quantum theory inspires some thinkers to reconsider how intelligence might emerge—not as a set of discrete rules, but as a process sensitive to context, uncertainty, and relational dynamics. Ideas like entanglement and observer dependence suggest new metaphors for cognition and perception. In parallel, quantum computing pushes AI toward radically different architectures, where parallelism and ambiguity are not bugs, but features.
